# Setting up Borderless Publishing: checklist for New South Wales

What a small publisher in Sydney needs in place, in a sensible order. Written in November 2026 for a sole trader. Fees and thresholds change: the item names the official place to check each one. Items marked **ask your accountant** or **ask a lawyer** are ones where the right answer depends on your circumstances.

## 1. Name and identity

- [ ] **Check the name is free.** Search "Borderless Publishing" on the ASIC business name register (asic.gov.au, "Search business names"), on the Australian Trade Mark Search of IP Australia (ipaustralia.gov.au), and on the WIPO Global Brand Database for overseas trade marks. "Borderless" is a common word, so look for publishers and booksellers with similar names before you invest in printing.
- [ ] **ABN.** Apply for an Australian Business Number as a sole trader at abr.gov.au. It is free.
- [ ] **Business name.** Because "Borderless Publishing" is not your own name, it must be registered with ASIC before you trade under it. Register for one or three years and note the renewal date in the calendar.
- [ ] **Web address.** A `.com.au` address needs an ABN or a registered business name; register the domain in the business name. Also consider the `.com`.
- [ ] **Email** on that domain (for example hello@ and your own name). Fill it in `01-brand/source/brand-details.json` and rebuild the business card.
- [ ] **Trade mark (optional, later).** Registering the name and logo with IP Australia protects them in Australia. Typical classes for a publisher: 16 (printed books), 9 (ebooks and audiobooks) and 41 (publishing services). There is a fee for each class; check the current fee on the IP Australia site. Worth doing once the name is settled and you have published.

## 2. Money and tax

- [ ] **Separate bank account** for the business, used for every platform payout, every cost and every donation. It makes royalty statements, tax and the donation statement simple and trustworthy.
- [ ] **Records.** Keep every payout report, invoice, receipt and statement for five years (ATO record keeping rule). The money tracker in this folder is a start; a simple accounting app is better once there are several titles.
- [ ] **Income tax.** As a sole trader, business income and costs go in your own tax return. **Ask your accountant** how the ATO will treat a publishing activity that deliberately aims to make little or no profit: it affects whether losses can be offset against other income (the non-commercial losses rules) and whether the ATO sees it as a business at all.
- [ ] **GST.** Registration is required once business turnover reaches the GST threshold (75,000 dollars a year at the time of writing; check ato.gov.au). Below it, you may choose not to register; then you charge no GST and your invoices must not be called tax invoices. Platform payouts from overseas count towards turnover.
- [ ] **United States tax forms.** Complete the tax interview on Amazon KDP, IngramSpark and Draft2Digital as an Australian, so that US withholding on your royalties is reduced to the treaty rate. Each platform's interview shows the rate it will apply. Any US tax withheld may be claimed as a foreign income tax offset: **ask your accountant**.
- [ ] **The donations and tax.** Gifts to an overseas organisation such as Blessings Foundation Bangladesh are generally not tax deductible in Australia, because it is not a deductible gift recipient. That means you may pay income tax on the margin first and donate from what is left. **Ask your accountant** whether a better route exists, for example giving through an Australian organisation endorsed under the Overseas Aid Gift Deduction Scheme that works with the Foundation, and how to show the donations in your records.
- [ ] **Paying people overseas.** Royalties and fees paid to an author or translator who lives outside Australia can be subject to Australian withholding tax. **Ask your accountant** before the first payment.

## 3. The promise to donate

Your books and website say that your share of any margin is donated. That is a public statement about money, so it needs care.

- [ ] **Agree the arrangement in writing** with Blessings Foundation Bangladesh (letter 06 in the agreements folder). Get their full legal name, registration details, bank account and an approved description of their work.
- [ ] **Keep it true and checkable.** Under the Australian Consumer Law, statements about where money goes must not be misleading. Say exactly what is donated ("our share of the margin after direct costs and author royalties"), donate on a fixed schedule, keep the Foundation's receipts, and publish a yearly statement (template in 04-donation-and-transparency-policy).
- [ ] **Fundraising rules.** In New South Wales, selling goods with a statement that proceeds go to a charitable purpose can be a fundraising appeal under the Charitable Fundraising Act 1991, which may need an authority. **Ask NSW Fair Trading or a lawyer** whether your arrangement needs one before you print the statement on books. Ask the same for other states if you sell there in person.
- [ ] **Words to avoid** about yourself: "not for profit", "charity" and "registered charity". Borderless Publishing is a business that donates; say it that way.

## 4. Publishing infrastructure

- [ ] **ISBNs.** You own ISBNs from Thorpe-Bowker (myidentifiers.com.au). Use one for each format (paperback, hardcover, large print, ebook, audiobook), registered in the name Borderless Publishing, and record each in the ISBN register in Borderless Studio. Fill in the title details at Thorpe-Bowker so the book appears in Books in Print.
- [ ] **Platform accounts in the business name**: Amazon KDP, IngramSpark, Draft2Digital, with the business bank account and the tax interviews done.
- [ ] **Legal deposit.** Australian law requires a copy of every book published in Australia to be given to the National Library of Australia within one month of publication. Ebooks and print are deposited through National edeposit (ned.gov.au), which also serves the state libraries; for printed books, New South Wales law also asks for a copy for the State Library of NSW. Check the current process on the National Library's legal deposit page for each format. The publishing checklist in Borderless Studio includes this step.
- [ ] **Lending rights.** Register as a publisher for Public Lending Right and Educational Lending Right, run by the Australian Government (Office for the Arts). Publishers and creators are paid each year for eligible books held in public and school libraries. New titles must be claimed by the yearly closing date (31 March at the time of writing; check the Lending Rights page).
- [ ] **Copyright Agency.** Join as a publisher member (copyright.com.au) so you are paid when your books are copied under education and government licences. Membership is free.
- [ ] **Cataloguing data.** Ask the National Library whether a prepublication record is available for your books (the app can print the catalogue line on the copyright page).

## 5. Risk

- [ ] **Contracts reviewed** by a lawyer before the first use (see the agreements folder).
- [ ] **Defamation and privacy.** Memoir and nonfiction that name real people carry the most risk for a publisher. For such books, budget for a legal read before publication.
- [ ] **Insurance.** Ask an insurance broker about publisher's or media liability insurance (defamation and copyright claims) once you publish other authors, and public liability if you sell at markets and festivals.
- [ ] **Permissions.** Keep every signed permission with the book's files (letter 07).
- [ ] **Backups.** Borderless Studio makes daily zip backups; set the backup folder to a drive or cloud folder that is not your laptop.

## 6. Reader and author relations

- [ ] **Privacy.** Small businesses under the Privacy Act turnover threshold are generally exempt, but authors and readers will trust you more if your website has a short privacy statement saying what you collect (names, emails, bank details of authors) and that you never sell it.
- [ ] **Newsletter.** Under the Spam Act 2003 every email newsletter needs the reader's consent, your name and contact details, and a working unsubscribe link. Use a mailing service that handles this.
- [ ] **Submissions.** Publish the submission guidelines (04-authors) and reply to every submission, even with a short no.

## 7. Communities worth joining

- **Small Press Network** (smallpressnetwork.com.au): the association of Australian independent publishers. Industry news, events, and the Independent Publishing Conference.
- **Australian Society of Authors**: you are an author too; and their guidance shows what other authors will expect of you as a publisher.
- **Writing NSW** (writingnsw.org.au): courses, events and a community of New South Wales writers, a good place to meet authors.
- **Arts Law Centre of Australia**: affordable legal help for creative businesses.
