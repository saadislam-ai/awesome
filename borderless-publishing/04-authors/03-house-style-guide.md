# House style

How Borderless books are written and set. Editors and proofreaders follow this guide; authors may ask for exceptions for a particular book, which we note on the book's style sheet. Borderless Studio applies most of these rules automatically and flags the rest.

## Spelling

- **Australian English**, following the Macquarie Dictionary: colour, realise, organise, travelled, program (for software), programme (for events).
- A book may use British or American spelling if the author prefers; it is then set in Borderless Studio (Book, Details, Spelling) and used throughout.
- Bangla: standard spelling (Bangla Academy). Names transliterated from Bangla keep one spelling throughout the book, recorded in the story bible.

## Dashes

**We do not use em dashes or en dashes.** Where another publisher would use one:

| Instead of | Use |
| --- | --- |
| A dash around an aside | Commas, or brackets |
| A dash before an explanation | A colon |
| A dash for interrupted speech | An ellipsis ("I was going to..."), or recast |
| A range of numbers or dates | "to" in running text (pages 12 to 18, 1971 to 1975), or a hyphen in tables and references (12-18) |

The hyphen is used only inside compound words (well-worn, half-forgotten). Borderless Studio's dash check finds every dash before the book is exported.

## Quotation marks and punctuation

- Double curly quotation marks for speech and quotations, single inside double. [Or single first, as some Australian fiction prefers; decide per book.]
- Punctuation goes inside the quotation marks when it belongs to the quoted words.
- No serial (Oxford) comma, unless it is needed to avoid ambiguity: red, white and blue.
- One space after a full stop.
- The ellipsis is a single character (…), with a space after it before a following word and none before it.

## Numbers and dates

- Words for numbers below ten, and for round numbers in fiction (a hundred people); figures for 10 and above, measurements, money and percentages.
- Dates: 26 March 1971. Decades: the 1970s.
- Money: 20 dollars in fiction, $20 in nonfiction; other currencies as Tk 500, US$20.

## Names, titles and words from other languages

- Mr, Mrs, Dr, St without a full stop.
- Words from Bangla and other languages are not set in italics unless the author wishes; a glossary at the back is preferred to footnotes in fiction.
- Book titles in italics; chapter and article titles in quotation marks.

## Notes and references (nonfiction)

- Footnotes for comments, numbered by chapter; endnotes for sources in books with many references.
- References: Author, *Title* (Publisher, City, Year), page.

## Layout (set in Borderless Studio)

- Body: EB Garamond, 11 point on 14.5 point (paperback 5.5 x 8.5 in), justified, hyphenated, first line of each paragraph indented except after a heading or scene break.
- Scene breaks: a centred ornament or blank line, as chosen for the book; never at the top of a page without the ornament.
- Chapters start on a right hand page in nonfiction, on any page in fiction.
- Large print editions: 16 point or larger, ragged right, no hyphenation (checked against the large print guidance file).
- Bangla: Noto Serif Bengali or Tiro Bangla, Bengali numerals for page numbers.

## Front and back matter

Half title, title page, copyright page (with the ISBN of the edition and the National Library catalogue line where available), dedication, epigraph, contents (nonfiction and long fiction), the text, acknowledgements, about the author, also by the author.
